How Darby Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which limits scalding risk and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on descaling every year in hard-water areas.

The age of your heater matters just as much. With regular maintenance, a tank water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like rust-colored water, rumbling, or moisture under the tank, it's time to plan a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule costs less and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new heater with water spreading across the floor.

The sooner you call about a hot water problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A sediment layer that's just starting to rumble can often be fixed with a flush, yet the same sediment, neglected for a few years, can overheat the bottom of the tank and shorten the life of the tank, turning a simple visit into a replacement. Save our number on your phone today so you can reach us fast as soon as something sounds or looks wrong. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

A water heater in Darby rarely fails without warning. Watch for a popping or rumbling sound during heating, brownish hot water, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, a damp spot under the tank, or hot water that runs out a little sooner each month. These are early signs of heavy sediment, a depleted anode, a weak element or burner, and early corrosion, and caught early, they're usually an affordable repair or a planned replacement. Ignored, they lead to a ruptured tank and a cleanup bill. Pay close attention to a relief valve that drips or discharges: it means the tank is seeing too much pressure or temperature, and it should be checked promptly. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so small problems stay small.
